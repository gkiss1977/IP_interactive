Ringing Artifacts & The Gibbs Phenomenon

Why Ringing Happens:

An Ideal Low-Pass Filter has a sharp vertical step discontinuity at cutoff $D_0$. By the Convolution Theorem, multiplication in frequency space corresponds to convolution with the spatial impulse response $h(x,y)$.

H(u,v) = rect(D / 2D₀) ⟷ h(x,y) = sinc(D₀ · r)

The spatial sinc kernel contains infinite decaying side-lobes (ripples). Convolving sharp edges with these side-lobes produces overshoot and undershoot oscillations known as Ringing.

Suppressing Ringing (Smooth Filters):

To eliminate spatial ripples, the frequency transfer function $H(u,v)$ must transition smoothly without sharp edges.

  • Gaussian Filter: The Fourier transform of a Gaussian is another Gaussian ($e^{-r^2} \leftrightarrow e^{-k u^2}$). It has zero side-lobes, guaranteeing 0% ringing.
  • Butterworth Filter: Allows a smooth trade-off. Order $n=1,2$ has negligible ringing, while order $n \ge 8$ approaches the ideal step and induces ringing.

Comprehensive Filter Type Comparison Table

Ideal Filter Severe Ringing

Truncates abruptly at $D_0$. Infinite spatial side-lobes result in severe Gibbs oscillations around image edges.

H(D) = 1 if D ≤ D₀ else 0
Butterworth Tunable Ringing

Smooth transition controlled by order $n$. Low orders ($n=1,2$) eliminate ringing; high orders ($n \ge 8$) approach step cutoff.

H(D) = 1 / [1 + (D/D₀)²ⁿ]
Gaussian Zero Ringing

Smooth bell-curve in both frequency and spatial domains. Inverse FT of a Gaussian is a Gaussian, guaranteeing zero ripples.

H(D) = exp(-D² / 2D₀²)
Raised Cosine Minimal Ringing

Smooth sinusoidal roll-off. Widely used in telecom/signal processing to minimize inter-symbol interference and spatial ringing.

H(D) = ½[1 + cos(π D / D₀)]
